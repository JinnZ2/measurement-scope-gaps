## 2. Base assumptions per reading (DERIVED from method; CHECK in papers)

**Heat-flux proxy (Foukal 2025)**
- flux ↔ AMOC relation stationary (calibrated in models, applied to observations)
- atmosphere does not reshape flux independently (ENSO, NAO, aerosols)
- model meltwater ≈ real meltwater

**Arrays (Apr 2026)**
- ~20 yr separates trend from decadal variability
- east + west coverage captures the net

**Observational constraint (Apr 2026)**
- present-day salinity bias stationary to 2100
- "matches observations" selects the right controls
- meltwater forcing as the models have it

**Long runs (Drijfhout et al. 2025)**
- the few models run to 2300/2500 represent the ensemble
- ice sheets prescribed or absent, not interactive

**Shared across the three model-based readings:** meltwater forcing set by the
models, not by observed melt. If observed melt exceeds modeled melt, all three
err the same direction — weakening too small, collapse too late. Agreement
among readings that share a convention is not independent evidence.

## 4. Prior strong El Niños and step-like warming

- 1976–77: North Pacific regime shift (PDO flip, ecosystem change), widely described as a regime shift (CHECK: Hare & Mantua 2000; [regimeshifts.org #406](https://regimeshifts.org/item/406-north-pacific-ocean))
- 1997–98: [Jones & Ricketts 2016](https://vu-9.eprints-hosting.org/33853/1/39_Jones%26Ricketts_2016_Atmospheric_Warming_1997-2014.pdf) — 39 of 45 temperature records show a step in 1996–98; global step ≈ +0.32°C; post-step trends 0.06–0.11°C/decade; prior global step 1979/80; flagged 2014–16 as a possible new step
- 2015–16: ended the 2001–14 slowdown; [Trenberth](https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/news/2023/07/25/global-temperatures.html) describes warming as a staircase — La Niña pauses the rise, temperatures do not fall back

Two readings of the same record: smooth trend + noise (dominant) vs stable
regimes punctuated by steps (minority). OBSERVED: neither predicts return to
the pre-event level. They disagree on shape (ramp vs staircase), not direction.

DERIVED: return to a pre-event level would need forcing to drop. El Niño moves
stored deep heat to the surface and atmosphere; the heat does not return to
depth on the event's timescale. With subsurface heat at a record, there is no
mechanism on the table for return to a prior baseline.

PROPOSED (user's): the 2026 event is a phase change. Filed as a hypothesis with
the tests below.

## 5. Open study designs

**A5 — Observed-melt forcing.** Rerun model AMOC estimates with observed
Greenland melt as forcing; report the shift in weakening % and collapse timing.
If warming steps rather than ramps, also force with a stepped series and
compare threshold-crossing times against smooth forcing.

**A6 — Pre-registered response to the 2026 El Niño.** Before the response
arrives, state which measurand (heat flux, array transport, cold-blob SST,
salinity) should move, in which direction, with what lag. The split between
instruments during the event tests the stationarity assumptions above.

**A7 — Critical slowing before 2026 (desk study).** Rolling autocorrelation and
variance on the pre-2026 ENSO subsurface record. Rising both → system losing
resilience before the event; flat → abrupt forcing, not slowing.

**A8 — Define "baseline return" now.** For subsurface heat, SOI, Niño 3.4:
write the return criterion and scoring date before the event ends. Two versions:
return to prior level, return to prior trend. Score both.

**A9 — Coupled-window register.** Cold blob, Greenland melt, ENSO, AMOC arrays
on one timeline. Do excursions line up, lag, or stay independent? Lined-up
excursions across subsystems are the signature to look for in a coupled shift.

**A10 — Ramp vs staircase as a reading split.** Fit both models to the same
records (global, regional, subsurface). Report where they disagree in timing of
predicted threshold crossings; that disagreement locates the cases where the
choice of reading changes the policy-relevant answer.
